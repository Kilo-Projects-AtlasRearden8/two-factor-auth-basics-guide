Recovery Codes

Your emergency spare key when you can’t get your usual 2FA code.

Start here › Recovery codes

Recovery codes are your emergency spare key. When you turn on 2FA, most websites give you a set of one-time codes (usually 8–10) to save somewhere safe. If you ever lose your phone, delete your authenticator app, or can’t get a text, you use one of these codes to get back in.

Why recovery codes matter

Without recovery codes, losing access to your 2FA method can mean losing access to your account forever. Some accounts can be recovered through customer support, but many cannot — especially free accounts. Recovery codes are your safety net.

What to do with recovery codes

  1. Copy or print them immediately when the website shows them. You usually only see them once.
  2. Store them somewhere safe and separate from your phone. Good options: a printed sheet kept in a locked drawer or safe at home; a locked, fireproof box; a password manager that you protect with its own strong password and 2FA.
  3. Don’t store them only on the phone you’re protecting. If the phone is lost or broken, the codes are gone too.
  4. Don’t email them to yourself as plain text. Your email is often the account you’re trying to protect.
  5. Don’t save them in a plain text note or screenshot that anyone who picks up your phone can read.

How to use a recovery code

  1. Try to log in with your password and 2FA code as usual.
  2. When you can’t get your 2FA code, look for a link like “Try another way,” “Use a recovery code,” or “Lost your phone?”
  3. Enter one of your saved recovery codes. It works once, then it’s used up.
  4. Once you’re in, go to security settings and generate a new set of recovery codes (the old one is now partially used, and you want a full fresh set).

When you use a recovery code, cross it off your list so you don’t try it again. When you’re down to your last few codes, generate a new set right away.
