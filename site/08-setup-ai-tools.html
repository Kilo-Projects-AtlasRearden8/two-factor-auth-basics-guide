Step-by-Step: AI Tools

Protect your AI accounts like you would email or banking.

Start here › Set up: AI tools

AI tools (chatbots, image generators, writing assistants) often hold your conversation history, uploaded files, and sometimes payment info. Protect them like you would email or banking — especially if you use them for work, writing, or personal projects.

This chapter covers general steps that apply to most AI tools, plus notes for a few common ones.

General AI tool setup steps

  1. Log in to the AI tool’s website or app.
  2. Go to Settings → Account → Security (or “Privacy & security”).
  3. Look for Two-factor authentication, Two-step verification, or Login security.
  4. Turn it on and choose Authenticator app.
  5. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app, or copy the setup key and paste it in.
  6. Enter the six-digit code from the app to confirm.
  7. Save your recovery codes — print them or store them in your password manager.

Notes for common AI tools

  • ChatGPT / OpenAI: Go to Settings → Security → Two-factor authentication. Choose “Authenticator app.” Save the backup codes OpenAI shows you.
  • Claude / Anthropic: Check Settings → Security for two-factor authentication options.
  • Gemini / Google AI: These use your Google Account security, so follow the Gmail steps in the email chapter.
  • Microsoft Copilot: Uses your Microsoft account — follow the Outlook steps in the email chapter.

AI-specific tips

  • If you pay for an AI subscription, protect that account especially well — someone who gets in could make charges or use your credits.
  • Don’t paste passwords, API keys, or personal data (like addresses or ID numbers) into AI conversations, even with 2FA on. 2FA protects your login — it doesn’t make what you type private.
  • If you use AI tools for work, check with your employer before turning on 2FA or saving recovery codes on a work device.
