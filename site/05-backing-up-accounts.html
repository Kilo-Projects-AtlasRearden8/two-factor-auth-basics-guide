Backing Up Your Accounts

So you can still get in if your phone is lost, stolen, broken, or replaced.

Start here › Backing up accounts

Backing up 2FA means making sure you can still get into your accounts if your phone is lost, stolen, broken, or replaced. It’s not about backing up your data — it’s about backing up your access.

The golden rule

Never rely on a single phone as the only way to prove who you are. If that phone dies, your accounts die with it — unless you have backups.

Three layers of backup

Layer 1: Recovery codes (every account)

Save the recovery codes for every account you protect with 2FA (see the recovery codes chapter). This is the most important backup.

Layer 2: Authenticator app backup (if your app offers it)

Some authenticator apps can back up your account codes:

  • Microsoft Authenticator can back up to your Microsoft account
  • Authy can back up and sync across devices
  • Aegis (Android) can export an encrypted backup file
  • Google Authenticator can transfer accounts to a new phone

Check your app’s settings for “backup,” “sync,” “export,” or “transfer.” If it’s available, turn it on — but still keep recovery codes as your ultimate fallback.

Layer 3: More than one device (for important accounts)

For critical accounts (banking, primary email), consider:

  • Adding a second phone number as a backup (not your only method)
  • Adding a security key and keeping a spare key in a safe place
  • Keeping printed recovery codes in a secure location a trusted family member knows about (in case of emergency)

What to do when you get a new phone

  1. Set up 2FA on the new phone before you give away or wipe the old one.
  2. Transfer your authenticator accounts (using the app’s transfer feature) or re-scan each QR code.
  3. Test that you can log in to each account from the new phone.
  4. Only then, remove the old phone from your account’s trusted devices list.

Don’t forget your email account

Your email is often the key to every other account (password resets go there). Protect your primary email with 2FA first, and keep its recovery codes especially safe.
