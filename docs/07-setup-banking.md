# Step-by-Step: Banking

Banking accounts hold your money, so they deserve strong protection. Most banks offer 2FA, though they sometimes call it "multifactor authentication," "security codes," or "two-step verification."

Bank websites vary widely. If your bank's screens don't match exactly, look for "Security," "Privacy & security," or "Sign-in & security" in settings, or call your bank and ask how to turn on two-factor authentication.

---

## Before you start

- Have your bank's mobile app installed, or be at a computer where you can log in to online banking.
- Have your phone handy.
- Know that some banks *require* a text message or phone call and don't offer an authenticator app. That's still better than no 2FA.
- Never give your 2FA code to anyone who calls you — your bank will never ask for it over the phone or by text.

---

## Common bank setup steps (general)

1. Log in to your bank's website or mobile app.
2. Go to **Settings** → **Security** (or "Profile & settings" → "Security").
3. Look for **Two-factor authentication**, **Multifactor authentication**, **Two-step verification**, or **Security settings**.
4. Turn it on.
5. Choose your method:
   - **Authenticator app** (best, if offered): scan the QR code with your app.
   - **Text message:** enter your phone number, then enter the code the bank texts you.
   - **Phone call:** the bank calls you with a code.
   - **Security key:** some banks support a physical security key.
6. Confirm by entering the code you receive.
7. **Save any backup codes** the bank gives you. Print them and keep them somewhere safe.
8. Set up a backup method if the bank offers one (for example, a second phone number).

---

## What to watch for with banking

- **Some banks only text codes.** If that's your bank, turn it on anyway — it stops most attacks. Ask if they plan to add authenticator app support.
- **Some banks call you** with an automated voice code. Answer the call and enter the code on the website.
- **Don't use the same password** for your bank as for any other account.
- **Check your bank's app for login notifications** — many will alert you when someone logs in from a new device.
- **Never log in to banking on public Wi-Fi** without a VPN, and never share your screen while banking.

## If you get locked out

Call your bank's customer service using the number on the back of your debit card or on the bank's official website. Don't call a number someone texted or emailed you — it could be a scammer.
